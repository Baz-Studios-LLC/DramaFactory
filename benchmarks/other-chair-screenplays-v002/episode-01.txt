THE OTHER CHAIR
EPISODE 01 — NOT YOUR LINE
Assistant-authored development draft
Target: approximately 75 seconds. Mostly 1–4-second coverage; final timing to be measured with performances.

INT. SLOANE'S SECONDHAND — DISPLAY LIVING ROOM — AFTERNOON

TIGHT ON EMMA CLARKE, 25. Beautiful, well dressed, gripping her phone a little too hard. She faces somebody we cannot yet place.

EMMA
I don't want you at my wedding.

REVERSE: NORA FINCH, 25. Her expression collapses. For a moment, the wound looks real.

NORA — AS EMMA'S MOTHER
After everything I've done for you?

EMMA
I'm sorry. I just—

Nora raises one finger. The wounded mother disappears.

NORA
Stop.

Emma freezes.

NORA
You just invited me back.

A beat. Emma looks from Nora to the empty space between their chairs.

EMMA
What?

NORA
Try it without apologizing.

We finally WIDEN. This is a furniture showroom: three pretend living rooms, price tags, an amber lamp. The apparent family argument is a rehearsal.

LEAH SLOANE, 26, crosses behind Nora carrying a SOLD tag.

LEAH
You're on a sold chair.

NORA
She's having a breakthrough.

LEAH
She's leaving an imprint.

Nora gets up without breaking eye contact with Emma. Leah slides the tag onto the chair. The commerce continues around the emotional emergency.

Emma exhales. Tries again. Less performative. More frightened, and braver.

EMMA
I want you there.

Nora lets the sentence land.

EMMA
But not if you spend the day punishing me.

For once, Nora has no clever improvement.

NORA
There she is.

EMMA
And then what do I say?

NORA
You let her answer.

Emma looks at her phone. She does not make the call yet. She has found the words; using them is her decision.

She puts a twenty-dollar bill on the side table.

EMMA
I only came in for a lamp.

Nora glances at the amber lamp beside them.

NORA
You should still get the lamp.

Leah is already lifting its price tag. Emma laughs despite herself.

AT THE FRONT DOOR

Emma leaves with her phone and a lamp receipt. She turns back.

EMMA
Thank you.

Nora almost answers in the mother's voice. Catches herself.

NORA
Good luck.

Emma goes. Leah picks up the twenty.

LEAH
Twenty dollars. Ten minutes?

NORA
Some people just need the other chair.

Leah studies Nora, then the suddenly useful display room.

LEAH
Half.

NORA
Of the twenty?

LEAH
Of the business.

Nora smiles. She has accidentally found a job.

Her phone VIBRATES. BEN.

The smile changes before she answers: a brighter, easier version, ready for somebody she loves.

NORA
Hey.

BEN — PHONE
I got the job. Two weeks.

A beat.

BEN — PHONE
Tell me we're doing this.

CLOSE ON NORA.

The woman who knew exactly what Emma should say cannot find her own answer.

CUT TO BLACK.
